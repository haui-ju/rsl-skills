## Abstract
<a id="abstract"></a>

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI) represents a significant shift in the way peo- ple interact with technology, with a growing impact in sectors such as education, communication, and content creation. Although its applications promise relevant benefits for people with cognitive disabilities, such as language simplification or support for task organization, there are also significant accessibility challenges that remain unexplored. This paper presents the results of a systematic literature review focused on the cognitive accessibility of textual GAI interfaces. The main objec- tive was to identify the current cognitive accessibility challenges inherent in textual GAI interfaces and to synthesize the solutions proposed to promote a more inclusive interaction. To this end, seven studies selected between 2020 and 2024 from four academic databases (Scopus, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore, and ACM Digital Li- brary) were analyzed. The analysis reveals that current interfaces impose excessive cognitive load and suffer from systemic lack of predictability and user scaffolding due to their reliance on complex prompt engineering and low explainability. Emerging proposals based on inclusive design, personalization, multimodal interfaces, and eth- ical frameworks are also identified. The paper concludes by highlighting the need for more empirical studies, specific design guidelines, and validated evaluation methods to ensure that generative technologies are truly accessible to all.

1. Introduction and Related Work

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI) is defined by Lim et al. (2023) as “a technology
that (i) leverages deep learning models to (ii) generate human-like content (e.g., images,
words) in response to (iii) complex and varied prompts (e.g., languages, instructions,
questions)”. In recent years, GAIs have significantly influenced many areas of daily life,
transforming how we search for information, write, program, and even perform research.

GAI tools hold significant promise for people with cognitive disabilities. For students
with communication disabilities, such as dyslexia or cerebral palsy, GAI could facilitate
written communication by offering spelling and grammar correction, sentence rephras-
ing, and even full-text generation (de Haas, 2023; Heidt, 2024; Bradshaw, 2023). It can
also help neurodiverse students understand complex texts by summarizing, simplifying,
and clarifying textual content (Heidt, 2024; Leos, 2023). In addition, people with Atten-
tion Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) may benefit from customizable reminders,
motivational prompts, and support in organizing daily routines (Leos, 2023).
   The emergence of GAI interfaces represents a fundamental paradigm shift in Human-
Computer Interaction (HCI), moving away from structured, menu-based systems toward
open-ended, prompt-based interactions (Nielsen, 2023). While the inclusive potential of
GAI is frequently highlighted, this new interaction model introduces significant cog-
nitive challenges that remain critically underexplored. Traditional interfaces provided
predictability through visible system status and predefined options, thereby managing
cognitive load. In contrast, GAI requires users to formulate abstract queries, interpret
non-deterministic responses, and iterate through trial and error (Tankelevitch et al.,
2023). Instructions might be confusing, visual interfaces look somehow chaotic with
visual noise, the way the information is presented imposes a significant extrinsic load
on people with cognitive disabilities and it is necessary to reduce it. The dependence
on complex prompt formulation reveals a systemic lack of scaffolding mechanisms and
inherently increases overall cognitive load, severely undermining predictability. Pre-
dictability is key to reducing extrinsic cognitive load, because when the user knows
what to expect, they do not need to invest working memory or mental effort in figuring
out how the system works (Daronnat et al., 2021). With appropriate scaffolding, the
task can be made more manageable by dividing it into steps and reducing its complexity
(intrinsic load); it can remove noise by reducing unnecessary decisions and clarifying the
task (extrinsic load); and it can increase understanding and guided interaction (germane
load) (Hollender et al., 2010).
   Despite these widespread structural problems, current literature has largely failed to
evaluate GAI interfaces using an HCI framework. To date, the few studies that assess
cognitive accessibility often focus only on isolated issues such as unreliable speech-to-
text input (Guo et al., 2020) or high output reading levels (Roegiest and Pinkosova,
2024). GAI systems must be evaluated using HCI frameworks based on the basic princi-
ples of cognitive accessibility before it can be determined whether the potential identified
in these applications for people with cognitive disabilities is close to becoming a reality.
Given that the new GAI paradigm systematically challenges core principles of cognitive
accessibility, the structural exclusion of users with disabilities from these emerging tech-
nologies is a significant and pressing concern. Accessibility is a fundamental prerequisite
for equitable participation in digital environments, and that exclusion from emerging
technologies such as GAI may exacerbate existing social and educational inequalities.
As Morris (2020) emphasizes, when digital systems are not designed with disability
in mind, they risk excluding entire communities from access to essential services. In
that sense, Alshaigy and Grande (2024) critically examine the persistent inequities be-
tween the rapid development of GAI and the accessibility needs of users, arguing these
tools are predominantly developed by able-bodied designers alongside the absence of
design guidelines specifically tailored for GAI accessibility. Addressing these persistent
conceptual and structural becomes a pressing issue for ensuring more accessible AI.

spective. In scoping review of the state of the art, Hemsley et al. (2024) report a positive
trend in the adoption of innovative methodologies such as GAI to elicit the perspectives

2. Methodology

To ensure a rigorous and transparent review process, this study followed a systematic
methodology based on the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews
and Meta-Analyses) statement (Moher et al., 2009), which provides a structured ap-
proach to help researchers report their reviews in a clear, transparent, and replicable
manner (Creswell and Clark, 2017). The aim was to identify and analyze scientific litera-
ture that addresses the cognitive accessibility of textual Generative AI (GAI) interfaces.
This systematic literature review (SLR) addresses a set of clearly defined research ques-
tions by analyzing peer-reviewed publications from 2020 to 2024, retrieved from four
major academic databases in the field of Computer Science: ACM, IEEE, Web of Science
(WoS), and Scopus.
   Following the PRISMA framework, we organized the review process into four sequen-
tial phases:
   • Identification: An automatic search was conducted using a query string designed
     to capture the key concepts related to the research questions across the selected
     databases.
   • Screening: The initial set of retrieved studies was filtered by reviewing titles,
     abstracts, and keywords to identify those that aligned most closely with the ob-
     jectives of the review.
   • Eligibility: Full-text versions of the shortlisted articles were assessed by authors
     to extract relevant categories and descriptors for in-depth analysis.
   • Inclusion: The final set of studies was selected based on a quality assessment
     and metadata analysis of the identified descriptors.

2.1. Research questions
Our systematic literature review was guided by the following research questions:

   • RQ1: What are the current design characteristics and the main cognitive or intel-
     lectual accessibility challenges (or barriers) identified in the literature regarding
     textual Generative AI interfaces?
   • RQ2: What solutions have been proposed to improve the accessibility of textual
     generative AI interfaces for individuals with cognitive disabilities?

   These questions aim to structure the review across two essential dimensions. RQ1 is
essential for understanding how generative AI interfaces are currently designed, identify-
ing both the predominant design patterns and the key barriers that users with cognitive
disabilities face when interacting with these technologies. RQ2 is critical for mapping
the proposed strategies and interventions aimed at enhancing accessibility in generative
AI interfaces, contributing to more inclusive and user-centered design approaches.


2.2. Eligibility Criteria
Study selection was guided by the PICOC (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Out-
comes, Context) framework (Carrera-Rivera et al., 2022), which is appropriate for struc-
turing systematic reviews in the field of human-computer interaction and accessibility.
This framework was applied as follows:

   • Population: Generative AI tools that have a user interface and generate textual
     content. This criterion allows us to focus on systems that involve direct interaction
     between the user and the AI model, which is essential for evaluating interface
     accessibility.
   • Intervention: Studies that explore or evaluate aspects related to the cognitive
     accessibility of these interfaces. Priority is given to studies that specifically address
     barriers, supports, or design proposals aimed at people with cognitive difficulties.
   • Comparison: Tools that do not incorporate cognitive accessibility criteria, used as
     a contrast or reference point when discussing design limitations or gaps.
   • Outcome: Identification of barriers, best practices, recommendations, or findings
     related to cognitive accessibility. This criterion seeks to ensure that the selected
     studies contribute relevant and applicable knowledge to this specific field.
   • Context: People with cognitive or intellectual disabilities who could benefit from
     the use of more accessible generative AI tools. This criterion reflects the overall
     objective of the study: to promote the inclusion of users with diverse cognitive
     needs.

     sors, which have led to the design of new interfaces focused on natural language.

publication had to include at least one term from each group to be considered in the
initial search results. The structure of the query followed the pattern: (1) AND (2) AND
(3).
   The final search string used for automatic querying across the four selected digital
libraries (ACM, IEEE, Web of Science, and Scopus) was constructed as follows:
  (“Generative AI” OR “Generative Artificial Intelligence”) AND (“User
  experience” OR “Interface” OR “Human Computer Interaction” OR “Usab*”
  OR “Accessib*”) AND (“impairment*” OR “Autis*” OR “Neurodivers*” OR
  “disab*”)
   This strategy was designed to capture literature at the intersection of generative AI
technologies, interface usability and accessibility, and cognitive or intellectual disabili-
ties. The process of refining the query was rigorous and iterative, aimed at balancing the
inclusion of relevant synonyms and keywords frequently used in the field, while account-
ing for the limitations of the search text fields in each database (as not all databases
support the use of wildcards such as the asterisk).


2.4. Study Selection Process
The study selection process followed the guidelines of the PRISMA framework, with
the aim of ensuring a rigorous, transparent, and replicable methodology. To efficiently
manage bibliographic references and organize the different phases of study screening,
we used Rayyan (Ouzzani et al., 2016), a free web-based tool designed specifically for
systematic and narrative reviews. Rayyan supports semi-automated abstract and ti-
tle screening, offers an intuitive interface, and facilitates collaboration among multiple
reviewers by enabling task distribution, custom labeling, and transparent tracking of
inclusion decisions.

tracted different metadata such as title, authors, publication year, and automatically
computed attributes such as article language. These data populated filterable facets in
the Rayyan interface, aiding the organization and filtering of results. We also established
a set of agreed hashtags (e.g., #no-cognitive-disability, #no-user-interface) to consis-
tently flag reasons for inclusion or exclusion, thus contributing to the transparency and
reliability of the review process.

3.1. Characteristics and Cognitive Accessibility Challenges in GAI
     interfaces (RQ1)
The reviewed articles describe an evolving landscape in which GAI interfaces are moving
towards a user-intent based interaction model (Duarte et al., 2024). In this paradigm,
users express what they want to achieve, and the system executes the steps it considers
as appropriate for delivering the result. This model simplifies certain processes but
inherently introduces excessive cognitive load (Acosta-Vargas et al., 2024a; Pierrès et al.,
2025; Roomkham and Sitbon, 2024). This overload frequently arises from long or dense
responses, complex vocabulary, and the rapid presentation of information, factors that
significantly hinder comprehension, especially for individuals with ADHD or attention
and memory difficulties, as they place a high load on working memory.
   The need to formulate precise and effective instructions (a skill commonly referred to
as prompt engineering) becomes a core skill in itself that not all users possess (Duarte
et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024). This requirement for complex prompt engineering repre-
sents a significant barrier, disproportionately affecting users with cognitive disabilities
and creating inequalities in access to the benefits of AI, as it reveals a lack of adequate
user scaffolding in the interface design (Duarte et al., 2024; Roomkham and Sitbon,
2024).
   A recurrent theme in the reviewed literature is the intrinsic complexity and low
transparency of GAI systems (Acosta-Vargas et al., 2024b; Duarte et al., 2024). Even
when the interfaces appear simple on the surface, the underlying algorithmic processes
are often opaque and unpredictable, limiting users’ ability to understand how deci-
sions are made. This lack of behavioral predictability violates the core HCI principle of
predictability and contributes to user confusion, mental fatigue, and mistrust (Pierrès
et al., 2025). Furthermore, this opacity is coupled with the risk of generating inaccu-
rate results or “hallucinations” which further compromises system predictability and
complicates the necessary task of verifying the accuracy of the generated information.
   In terms of interaction design, most interfaces are based on text or voice (Duarte
et al., 2024; Roomkham and Sitbon, 2024). While conversational design can facilitate
use for certain profiles, this limitation highlights the lack of flexibility for users who
require alternative communication styles, such as visual, symbolic, or simplified formats
(Liu et al., 2024; Haroon and Dogar, 2024). This reliance on text-based modalities
excludes nonverbal users and those with literal or visual thinking styles, representing a
failure to provide adequate scaffolding and input flexibility for diverse cognitive needs.
   Several studies point out that many of these tools have been developed without
explicitly considering users with cognitive disabilities, thereby reproducing structural
exclusions common in the broader history of digital interface design (Liu et al., 2024;
Duarte et al., 2024; Pierrès et al., 2025). Certain emerging features in tools such as
ChatGPT have demonstrated potential to support people with dyslexia, ADHD, or ASD
by offering summaries, text simplification, organizational assistance, or personalized
messages (Pierrès et al., 2025; Haroon and Dogar, 2024). However, these features are

3.2. Solutions proposed to improve the accessibility of textual GAI
     interfaces for individuals with cognitive disabilities (RQ2)
The solutions identified in the reviewed articles can be grouped into six major lines of
action aimed at enhancing the cognitive accessibility of GAI interfaces.
   The first line emphasizes the importance of inclusive design from the earliest stages
of design and development (Acosta-Vargas et al., 2024a,b; Duarte et al., 2024). This
involves integrating principles of accessibility, equity, and inclusion into interaction de-
sign, and ensuring the active participation of people with disabilities in both design
and evaluation processes (Pierrès et al., 2025; Acosta-Vargas et al., 2024a; Haroon and
Dogar, 2024; Liu et al., 2024).
   The second line defends the development of multimodal, intuitive, and explainable
interfaces. These systems should integrate diverse input and output modalities (voice,
text, images, gestures, simplified visualization) (Roomkham and Sitbon, 2024; Pierrès
et al., 2025) and promote iterative interaction, allowing users to refine outputs through
dialogue (Liu et al., 2024). Support tools like tutorials (Liu et al., 2024; Acosta-Vargas
et al., 2024a), clear confirmations, and content summaries are recommended (Acosta-
Vargas et al., 2024a). These actions will provide fundamental user scaffolding and reduce
the cognitive load of users. Furthermore, promoting transparency and explainability of
AI models (Duarte et al., 2024; Acosta-Vargas et al., 2024a; Haroon and Dogar, 2024)
is key to restoring predictability and increasing user understanding and autonomy.

4. Discussion

The core finding of this review is that cognitive accessibility remains a critical, yet
largely unmet, challenge in textual GAI interfaces. The current user-intent based inter-
action model, characterized by its reliance on prompt engineering, low explainability,
and text-centric modalities, violates core principles of cognitive HCI: it places an ex-
cessive cognitive load on users, severely compromises predictability, and demonstrates
a systemic lack of user scaffolding. These three inherent characteristics may undermine
usability for individuals with cognitive disabilities. While GAI has substantial potential
to support these users through features like simplification and organizational assistance,
these beneficial characteristics are often incidental, rather than systematically integrated
into inclusive design practices.
   The most critical gap lies between the identified challenges and the proposed solu-
tions. While the literature effectively maps the theoretical violation of cognitive load
management, predictability, and scaffolding, the proposed remedies – including multi-
modal and explainable interfaces, adaptive content, and the advocacy for ethical frame-
works and accessibility-aware policies (Duarte et al., 2024) – remain largely fragmented
and conceptual. These solutions are explicitly designed to restore these principles. For
instance, multimodal modalities improve comprehension (Liu et al., 2024; Roomkham

support guided learning. Together, the three characteristics may create accessible and
cognitively sustainable environments when they are conveniently integrated.


4.1. Strengths and Limitations
This review presents several notable strengths, particularly given the emerging nature
of the field. One of the primary strengths lies in its timely and specific focus on a highly
relevant but underexplored intersection: that of cognitive accessibility and GAI interface
design. As one of the first systematic efforts to explore this rapidly evolving domain,
this study provides a crucial foundational synthesis of the existing literature.
   By employing a rigorous methodology based on the PRISMA framework and drawing
from four major academic databases, the review offers a transparent and representa-
tive overview of current knowledge published between 2020 and 2024. Significantly, all
studies that met the inclusion criteria were published in 2024. This absence of relevant
publications in earlier years can be explained by the timeline of public engagement with
GAI technologies. Although foundational models like GPT-3 were introduced in 2020,
their implications for interface design and accessibility only became tangible following
the widespread adoption of tools like ChatGPT from late 2022 onward. In the initial
years, GAI was primarily explored in technical or conceptual terms, without concrete
applications that intersected with cognitive accessibility. As a result, academic inquiry
into this specific domain only began to emerge once GAI tools became part of everyday
digital experiences.
   In addition, the use of clearly defined and rigorous inclusion criteria, centered specifi-
cally on text-based GAI interfaces and cognitive accessibility, ensures the high relevance
and sharp focus of the selected studies. This precise scope enables an in-depth analy-
sis of specific challenges and emerging solutions, yielding actionable insights for both
researchers and practitioners. This systematic approach effectively maps the early land-
scape, identifying critical research gaps and providing a clear roadmap for future, more
robust investigations into GAI for cognitive diversity.
   Nevertheless, several limitations constrain the generalizability of the findings. First,
this systematic review is based on a limited number of studies (n = 7), which reflects
the early stage of research in this area.
   Second, the results are limited by the quality and methodological approaches of the
included studies. While all of them contribute to understanding the relationship be-
tween GAI and cognitive accessibility, they vary widely in scope and rigor. The studies
employ highly disparate approaches, such as automated audits (Acosta-Vargas et al.,
2024a,b), qualitative interviews (Liu et al., 2024; Pierrès et al., 2025), prototype evalua-
tions (Roomkham and Sitbon, 2024; Haroon and Dogar, 2024), and conceptual analyses
(Duarte et al., 2024).

4.2. Implications for Practice and Research
The results of this systematic review show that cognitive accessibility in GAI interfaces
is an emerging field, with multiple knowledge gaps and plenty of opportunities for future
research. Based on the analysis of the reviewed studies, the following priority lines are
proposed.
   First, there is an urgent need to conduct more empirical studies, particularly those fo-
cused on end-users with different types of cognitive or intellectual disabilities. Currently,
most studies include small samples, very specific profiles, or do not directly incorporate
people with disabilities in the evaluation processes. Conducting user studies, usabil-
ity tests, and longitudinal analyses in real-life contexts is essential to understand the
specific barriers faced by these groups and adapt technological solutions to their needs.
   Also, the development and validation of specific evaluation methods for cognitive
accessibility in GAI tools must be a priority. While standards such as WCAG exist,
their general and technical approach does not always allow for adequate assessment
of the key cognitive factors identified in this review: cognitive load, predictability, and
scaffolding (Acosta-Vargas et al., 2024a,b). In this sense, the guidelines developed by the
W3C’s COGA task force (COGA Working Group, 2020) represent a significant advance,
proposing specific recommendations for improving the cognitive accessibility of web
content and designs. However, these recommendations still need to be transformed into
systematic evaluation tools, with validated objective/subjective metrics and procedures
that include the direct experience of people with cognitive disabilities in their design and
application. This is especially critical in the context of GAI interfaces, whose complexity
and unpredictability require more specific and sensitive evaluation approaches.
   Another key line of research is the proposal and validation of specific design guidelines
for cognitive accessibility in GAI interfaces. Unlike other more established technologies,
GAI interfaces have specific characteristics, such as natural language-based interaction,
that require new guidelines adapted to the emerging paradigm. These guidelines should
be structured around HCI principles to ensure actionable design decisions. They should
specifically address how to minimize cognitive load through multimodal output and pre-
dictions to reduce choice overload, how to maximize predictability through clearer model
explainability, easy navigation and uniform terminology and how to embed scaffolding
mechanisms like prompt templates or contextual help (Liu et al., 2024; Roomkham and

   Finally, the importance of exploring ethical, regulatory, and educational dimensions
related to cognitive accessibility in GAI is also highlighted. This includes developing
ethical frameworks that protect user autonomy, designing inclusive institutional policies,
providing specific training for designers and developers in cognitive accessibility, and
creating regulations that guarantee inclusion from design to implementation (Duarte

   Taken together, these future directions point to the need to consolidate an interdis-

design, cognitive science, accessibility, and Artificial Intelligence. Only in this way it
will be possible to ensure that generative technologies are truly inclusive and respond
to the cognitive diversity of society.


5. Conclusions

This systematic literature review provides a structured and critical analysis of the cur-
rent state of cognitive accessibility in GAI interfaces, specifically focusing on text-based
natural language tools. The review addressed its primary goal by identifying the cur-
rent design landscape and associated cognitive challenges (RQ1), and synthesizing the
proposed solutions (RQ2).
   The findings reveal a significant gap: current GAI interface designs predominantly
are focused on neurotypical users, exhibiting low transparency and a language-based
interaction model that fails to adapt to diverse cognitive styles. The analysis highlights
that the current characteristics and main accessibility challenges (RQ1) are intertwined:
the design features – such as reliance on complex prompt engineering and the text-
centric modality – directly cause barriers, including excessive cognitive load, lack of
predictability, and poor system explainability.
   In response to the second research question (RQ2), the literature offers promising,
though incipient, lines for improvement. These proposed solutions point toward inclu-
sive approaches that prioritize adaptability, transparency, and user participation in the
design process, primarily through multimodal interfaces and personalized experiences
adapted to the user’s cognitive profile. However, these strategies often remain conceptual
or at the prototype stage, underscoring a critical research-to-practice gap.
   The main contribution of this article is to offer a critical and updated synthesis of
existing knowledge at the intersection of Generative AI and cognitive accessibility, a
field that is still underexplored but of great social relevance. We move beyond simple
literature mapping by systematically analyzing the identified challenges and proposed
solutions through the lens of core HCI principles (cognitive load, predictability, and
user scaffolding). This analytical focus provides a clear conceptual foundation for future
research and contributes actionable insights for the development of specific standards
and truly inclusive GAI technologies, by identifying both theoretical violations and
practical gaps.
