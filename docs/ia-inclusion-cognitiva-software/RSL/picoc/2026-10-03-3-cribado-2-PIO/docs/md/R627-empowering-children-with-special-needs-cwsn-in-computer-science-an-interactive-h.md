## Abstract
<a id="abstract"></a>

It is essential for teachers to effectively address the diverse needs of students within the classroom environment, encompassing variations in IQ, communication skills, problem-solving abilities, and mathematical proficiency. Ensuring the inclusion of CwSN (Children with Special Needs) is imperative to prevent social division in the classroom. While the department offers accommodations such as scribes, interpreters, or extra time for examinations, these measures may not fully address the specific needs of CwSN. This study explores the efficacy of employing hand gestures as a teaching tool for CwSN in computer science, particularly in the realm of number conversions. A machine learning-based tool has been developed and implemented to provide an inclusive and accessible approach, addressing the specific needs of students with learning and intellectual disabilities. Through a mixed-methods approach, the research aims to evaluate the impact of integrating hand gestures into the instruction of number conversions. This methodology offers a simplified and interactive approach, potentially enhancing comprehension, retention, and engagement among CwSN. By focusing on the mathematical aspects of the computer science syllabus, specifically number conversion, this method aims to streamline the learning process, requiring minimal arithmetic to perform conversions without the use of pen and paper. The hand gestures method significantly improved the effectiveness of teaching number conversions to Children with Special Needs (CwSN) in comparison to traditional approaches. This approach not only boosted their participation but also facilitated easier retention of the concepts.

    The effectiveness of incorporating hand gestures in facilitating the learning of number
conversions has garnered significant attention and interest due to its potential to enhance
comprehension and engagement among learners. Research indicates that integrating hand
gestures into instructional practices can serve as a powerful tool for reinforcing mathematical
concepts, particularly in complex topics like number conversions. Lee (2012) examined the
impact of gesture-based learning with virtual reality (GBLVR) on counting ability in three
children with Cerebral Palsy (CP), revealing significant improvements in counting skills from 1
to 10 through intuitive body movement interaction with specially designed GBLVR math courses.
Moreover, the kinesthetic nature of hand gestures stimulates multiple sensory modalities, catering
to diverse learning styles and enhancing overall learning outcomes. Furthermore, the interactive
and experiential nature of using hand gestures fosters active participation and encourages students
to actively engage with the material, promoting a deeper level of understanding. Overall, the
incorporation of hand gestures offers a promising approach to facilitate the learning of number
conversions, providing educators with a valuable tool to support student learning and academic
success in mathematics.

     Learning is a multifaceted process encompassing both mental engagement and physical
activity (Hannaford, 1995; Jensen, 2000). Physical movements complement cognitive functions,
providing additional avenues for processing information and facilitating learning. The working
memory plays a pivotal role in retaining and connecting transient information with real-world
experiences and mental perceptions (Atkinson & Shiffrin, 1968; Baddeley & Hitch, 1974;
Driscoll, 2005; Richey, 1986). As learners encounter real-world stimuli, they encode them,
fostering effective connections between working and long-term memory. Gestures can impact
how information is stored and recalled, offering alternative pathways for input and output during
information processing (Cook et al., 2010). Furthermore, early research suggests that bodily
engagement and environmental interactions may streamline cognitive processing by reducing the
burden on memory and simplifying cognitive tasks (Donald, 1991).

     An extensive examination was conducted on the role of gestures in foreign language
acquisition, affirming their significant contribution to learning. Macedonia and von Kriegstein
(2012) noted that the movements of the learner could enhance memory encoding, fostering
deeper and more robust retention of information. This encoded information, involving motor
imagery, not only penetrated deeply into memory but also improved its accessibility and
durability over time. Similarly, an embodied cognition framework was adopted to explore the
use of physical interactions in vocational learning. Their study made use of a Kinect technology-
enabled gesture recognition system to help cognitive impaired individuals with in completing
vocational tasks such as pizza making. By comparing baseline conditions without Kinect prompts
to intervention conditions with Kinect-enabled prompts, Chang et al. (2013) found that the
intervention significantly enhanced vocational performance. The impact of embodied interactions
was studied on mnemonic performance, employing Kinect technology to provide feedback during
memorization tasks. Participants who received feedback through Kinect demonstrated superior
immediate and delayed recall compared to those using traditional mouse input (Chao et al., 2013).
Furthermore, the effects of various embodied interactions were examined on language learning,
affirming the positive influence of embodiment on the learning process. A recurring theme across
these studies is the meaningful nature of body gestures and movements conveying information.
Such gestures construct motor imagery that represents and metaphorically encapsulates concepts,

4. Implementation and Tools Used

    Hand gestures method is done using Python language along with Mediapipe, Opencv, and

Phase 1 - Collecting the Image Data

    In Phase 1, we utilized the webcam of a laptop to capture 100 images for each gesture by

respective folders, with each folder representing a specific gesture. Considering the scenario

facilitated the creation of a diverse and comprehensive dataset for subsequent phases of the
project.

Phase 2 - Creating the Data Set

    In Phase 2, we focused on extracting the spatial patterns and geometry of the hand and
fingers to create a dataset for gesture recognition. We specifically considered the left hand and its
four fingers, aligning with the scenario of a 4-bit binary number representation. By analyzing the
spatial arrangement and geometrical features of the hand and fingers, we generated a dataset that
captures the essential characteristics necessary for accurate gesture recognition. This dataset was
then serialized and stored as a pickle file, ensuring easy accessibility and efficient utilization in
subsequent phases of the project.

     In Phase 3, we utilized the dataset generated in Phase 2 to train a random forest classifier for
gesture recognition. By feeding the spatial patterns and geometrical features of hand gestures into
the classifier, we trained it to accurately classify different gestures. After training, the classifier
successfully created a model capable of recognizing hand gestures with high accuracy. Evaluation
of the model revealed that it achieved a remarkable accuracy score of 100%, correctly classifying
all samples in the dataset. This exceptional performance demonstrates the effectiveness of the
random forest classifier in accurately identifying hand gestures based on the extracted features.
Additionally, the classifier provides the capability to generate classification results for new input
data, thereby facilitating real-time gesture recognition applications.
