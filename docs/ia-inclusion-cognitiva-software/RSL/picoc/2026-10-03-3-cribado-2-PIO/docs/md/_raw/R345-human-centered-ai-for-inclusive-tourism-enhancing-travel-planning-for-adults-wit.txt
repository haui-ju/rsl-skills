Human-centered AI for inclusive tourism: enhancing travel planning for
adults with autism spectrum disorder
Noemi Mauro∗ iD, Fabio Ferrero, Liliana Ardissono, Federica Cena
University of Torino, Corso Svizzera, 185, Torino, 10149, Italy




HI GHLIGH TS

• We found that, despite difficulties, autistic adults wish to plan trips autonomously.
• We propose a Human-Centered AI trip planner for mid/high-functioning ASD users.
• Step-by-step guidance helps autistic users plan trips with limited effort.
• The app empowers users to make choices while AI guides the process, ensuring balanced control.
• The app balances user guidance and decision-making control.




ARTICLE INFO                                                 ABSTRACT

Keywords:                                                    Travel experiences can challenge individuals with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) before embarking on a trip,
Autism                                                       because of the complexity of planning, but also during the trip, due to sensory overstimulation while visiting
Human-centered AI                                            places. Artificial Intelligence (AI) could help overcome some obstacles. However, it might represent a barrier
Recommender Systems                                          for people with mid-functioning autism if their needs for assistance are not considered when designing tourism
Planning                                                     applications. Moreover, most trip planners fully control the generation of solutions, undermining users’ decision
Tourism
                                                             autonomy.
                                                                 To address these challenges, we explored the development of itinerary planning technologies through a
                                                             human-centered approach. We aimed to balance guiding users in their decision-making with empowering them
                                                             to plan tours independently. The result is CARES, an Artificial Intelligence-driven trip planner designed for autis­
                                                             tic adults, which we present in this article. CARES applies a collaborative approach to developing itineraries,
                                                             based on the exploitation of (i) AI technologies for information filtering and interactive itinerary planning that
                                                             are robust to the data scarcity characterizing the autism domain; (ii) a user interface that reduces information
                                                             overload and decision fatigue in information exploration.
                                                                 We tested CARES with 14 autistic adults, gathering insights to guide future design and improve the usability
                                                             of Artificial Intelligence for neurodivergent users. The results indicate that our approach provides an accessible
                                                             solution for enhancing the travel experiences of mid- to high-functioning autistic adults. In doing so, it contributes
                                                             to more inclusive tourism.
